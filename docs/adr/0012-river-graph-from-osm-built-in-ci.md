# ADR-0012: River graph from OSM, built in CI

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-09-23
- **Source:** `docs/plan/ARCHITECTURE.md` §13 (recorded as a file in P0b; the plan is the source of truth)

## Context

Bifurcations are needed; the agent sandbox cannot reach Geofabrik or Overpass (research/map-rivers.md).

## Decision

OSM relations → osmium export → TypeScript graph → tippecanoe, run in `geo.yml`. Outputs are release assets, and the derived graph is published under ODbL. Agents develop against a committed fixture PBF. Station chainage comes from official river-km.

## Consequences

A monthly workflow and an ODbL download page.

## Amendment (P6a, 2026-10-02)

The P6a build (issue #21; `docs/plan/PHASES.md` §21) fixed the details the decision left open. Each point below was measured or checked on 2026-10-02.

- **Tool image, built in CI and never distributed.** `tools/geo/Dockerfile` builds libosmium 2.23.1, osmium-tool 1.19.1 and tippecanoe 2.79.0 from tag tarballs on codeload.github.com, each sha256-pinned in the Dockerfile's `ARG`s (`scripts/check-bom.ts` reads them; the BOM has a row each). Both stages start from the BOM's `node:26.10.0-trixie-slim` digest. osmium-tool 1.19.1 is a tag without a GitHub release (commit `18ac055a…`, 2026-04-07) and needs libosmium 2.23.1 or newer, so libosmium is built too (it is header-only). protozero, nlohmann-json, Boost and the compression libraries come from Debian trixie's signed apt and are not version-pinned (R-078). osmium-tool is GPL-3.0: the image is built in the CI job, used there and discarded. It is never pushed, uploaded or released, so no GPL distribution duty arises; the published assets are our own data (ODbL), not the tool. tippecanoe is in the image but unused until P6b. The image runs with `--network none --cap-drop ALL --security-opt no-new-privileges --read-only --tmpfs /tmp` as the runner's uid.
- **16 regions, not the plan's two French ones.** Geofabrik has no `grand-est` or `hauts-de-france` extract; the French extracts are the old regions `alsace`, `lorraine`, `champagne-ardenne` and `nord-pas-de-calais`, `picardie`. With NL, BE, LU, CH and the DE states BW, BY, HE, RP, SL, NW and NI that is 16 PBFs (about 7.4 GB). Every run checks the pinned paths against `index-v1-nogeom.json`. Downloads follow at most two same-host https redirects (`-latest` answers 307 to the dated file), check md5 fail-closed and record sha256.
- **No cache.** Each run downloads fresh. `scripts/check-workflows.sh` forbids `actions/cache` and Geofabrik rebuilds every file daily, so a cache would only serve stale or mixed extracts (owner decision, 2026-10-02).
- **Relations come from OPL.** `osmium export` does not export `type=waterway` relations, so membership is read with `osmium cat -t relation -f opl,add_metadata=false` and ways with `osmium export -f geojsonseq -a type,id,way_nodes`. Geofabrik cuts the regions at different moments (on 2026-10-02 Belgium was a day newer than the rest), so a border object can sit in two extracts in two versions: `extract.sh` merges with `osmium merge -H` (all versions) and keeps the newest valid version of each object with `osmium time-filter` (no time given), records each region's replication timestamp in the provenance, and refuses extracts more than 72 h apart (`extract_dates_too_far_apart`); the builder still fails on a duplicate way.
- **Signing and publish.** `rivernet-publish` runs on main only (schedule or dispatch). It is the one job with `contents: write` and `id-token: write`, has no checkout, signs each asset keyless with `cosign sign-blob`, verifies it against the identity `https://github.com/mwijkhuisen/Waterheight/.github/workflows/geo.yml@refs/heads/main` and creates `geo-<UTC date>` as a draft before it publishes. An existing release with the same `SHA256SUMS` is a no-op; different sums fail and nothing is rewritten. There is no environment approval, because nothing consumes the release before P6b's refresh script, which verifies the signature itself (R-080).
- **EU-Hydro is QA only.** The MapServer is EPSG:3857 (not 3035). The QA compares the digitised direction of our reaches with EU-Hydro segments, within a 300-request budget at one request a second, in CI only; it never fails the build and no EU-Hydro geometry is published. `qa-report.json` cites EU-Hydro v1.3 (© EU, Copernicus Land Monitoring Service) and the modification. Direction comes from the geometry (the matched piece of each segment, never its chord), because one `NEXTDOWNID` cannot express a bifurcation; Strahler is not used.
- **Wikidata evidence rule.** A river or canal enters `registry/rivers.yaml` or `registry/geo-sources.yaml` with a QID, a P402 relation where Wikidata has one and the evidence (query date and the mouth, P403, that tells namesakes apart: Nahe is Q168696, Lys is Q208493). The lookup is an opt-in script (refuses under CI, 80 requests at most), never part of a run. Rivers without a P402 (17) select ways by `wikidata` plus `name`, never by name alone, and the builder fails when a relation's `wikidata` tag differs from the registry (`wikidata_mismatch`).

## Amendment (P6b, 2026-10-03)

The P6b build (issue #21; `docs/plan/PHASES.md` §22) places the stations on the graph and publishes the river files. The decision stands (OSM relations, built in CI, ODbL download, agents work from a committed fixture); these points were fixed on 2026-10-03.

- **Placement is by name, never by distance alone.** The river of a station comes from a reviewed override (`registry/snap-overrides.yaml`), its published water body matched verbatim against `registry/rivers.yaml` `names` per source (for owner sources the `waters` table of the override file, which stays out of `rivers.yaml` and the database), or DE-1's river slug; the nearest edge of that river must lie within 500 m. No Wikidata matching (no station source publishes a QID) and no loose alias matching (the SPW "Orne" and the DE-7 "Aa" are namesakes). Canals are never edges, so a canal point is placed only through the override table and never on a river.
- **Chainage.** `km_official` is the operator's km (an override or DE-1), never derived. `km_graph` is the longest-path distance from the river's own sources and lives only in the generated registry file and `reaches-<ver>.json`; `station.km_system` stays the label of the official system (owner decision). `km_to_nl_entry` is positive outside the Netherlands, 0 at the entry node, negative below it, with five entry nodes (Lobith, Eijsden, the Scheldt border, the Dollard, the Vecht border); tributaries that enter NL on their own have none.
- **The graph is only as connected as OSM.** One reviewed join (the Scheldt gap, 541 m, routing only, never drawn) is in `rivers.yaml`; the other KG-161 gaps stay open and their stations get no entry node. A way kept by two relations belongs to the deepest river in the parent tree, so reaches never overlap, and no edge id is persisted across releases (KG-162).
- **Reaches are public.** They are cut at public stations only; owner and `off` stations are placed and given chainage but cut nothing and appear in no public output.
- **Outputs and licence.** A release adds `rivers-<ver>.pmtiles` (tippecanoe 2.79.0, z0 to z12, attribution in the metadata), `reaches-<ver>.json`, `rivers-<ver>.geojson.gz`, `snap-report.json` and `VERSION`. The graph, the tiles and the download are derived from OpenStreetMap under the ODbL 1.0 and carry "© OpenStreetMap contributors"; station ids and km are a separate collective database and are not under the ODbL (stated in the files' heads and the page footer). The download and the tiles hold no station data.
- **Serving is a host script.** `deploy/bin/rws-rivers-refresh` installs a signed release from the host (cosign against the `geo.yml` identity, sha256 lines, names from `VERSION` only, immutable names, a separate rivers manifest); no container gets egress. The generated `registry/rivernet.yaml` follows the committed fixture, while production serves the latest release, so they can differ until the fixture is refreshed (KG-164; the runbook's "Outputs, fixture refresh and the generated registry file").

## Amendment (P17a, 2026-10-18)

P17a (issue #65; `docs/plan/PHASES.md` §54, ADR-0022 decision 1) puts the Dutch delta and the gauged canals on the graph. The decision stands (OSM relations, built in CI, ODbL download, agents work from a committed fixture); these points were fixed on 2026-10-18.

- **The tidal zone is declared, never inferred.** `registry/rivers.yaml` marks 14 channels `kind: delta_channel` and `tidal_zone: true` (Boven-, Beneden- and Nieuwe Merwede, Noord, Oude Maas, Nieuwe Maas, Nieuwe Waterweg, Hollandse IJssel, Dordtsche Kil, Spui, Amer, Bergsche Maas, Hollandsch Diep, Haringvliet; owner, 2026-10-18). Their edges carry `direction: none` with `direction_reason: tidal_zone`, no travel time and no flow arrows. The acyclic check runs on downstream edges only, so loops are allowed inside the zone and a cycle anywhere else is still the fatal `cycle`. Het Scheur is a gaugeless directed `delta_channel` outside the zone with a whole-river `flags: tidal`, so it is hatched like the zone without being part of it. The tidal limits of the Lek, Waal and Maas stay working bounds, not a measurement.
- **Canals become edges (this revises P6b's "canals are never edges").** A `kind: canal` row (`direction: none`, `direction_reason: canal`) selects ways like a river and carries stations that need a name match to be placed. Canals stay in the raw graph assets (`river_graph.json`, `reaches.geojson` and the `unscored` list of `qa-report.json`: our ids and OSM geometry, no OSM name) and out of every product until P17b: `place()` drops canal edges and strips canal ids from the edges it keeps, so they reach no reaches file, tile, bin, download or snap report, and they cut no river's reaches. Canals take no label on the web.
- **Mouths.** A top-level `mouths` list names where a river ends in the sea or a lake: the node of the river's own edges nearest a curated point within `max_m`, else the fatal `mouth_unplaced`. The water body is one of `PENDING_WATERBODY_IDS` until P17b's file replaces the list.
- **Selection modes unchanged.** Exactly one of `osm_relation_id` (the river's own relation, first) or `osm_way_name` (ways with the QID tag, `waterway=*` and `name`). A third mode, the named members of the Rhine relation for side streams without a relation of their own, was planned and not kept: on the 2026-10-18 extract every delta channel and canal had its own relation or QID-tagged ways.
- **The canal QID rule.** A canal enters with its Wikidata item and the mouth (P403), or, where Wikidata has no P403, with a two-way P402 (the item points at the relation and the relation's `wikidata` tag points back). A canal with neither is deferred and listed in `docs/known-gaps.md`. The lookup (`lookup-wikidata.ts`) stays opt-in and refuses under CI; the P17a run took 18 requests against a budget of 80.
- **EU-Hydro scores directed edges only.** Edges with direction `none` are listed as `unscored` (with `unscored_count`) in `qa-report.json`, within the same bounding box and budget. `river_graph.json` and `reaches.geojson` are at `schema_version` 2; the public contract stays 1.

## Amendment (P17b, 2026-10-19)

P17b (issue #65; `docs/plan/PHASES.md` §54 "P17b as built", ADR-0022 decision 2) adds water bodies and canal stations to the same pipeline. The decision stands (OSM, built in CI, ODbL download, agents work from a committed fixture); these points were fixed on 2026-10-19.
- **Water bodies are a curated registry, extracted on their own.** `registry/waterbodies.yaml` holds lakes, closed estuaries, estuaries and seas; a lake or closed estuary is an OSM polygon (a multipolygon relation or a closed way), a sea or open estuary may be point-only (a label point and a reviewed sanity bbox, never drawn). `extract.sh` takes the polygons by OSM id from each region's download (`osmium getid -r`) before the download is deleted, because the waterway filter drops them, then merges and exports them (`--remove-tags`, `-a type,id --geometry-types=polygon`). They stay out of `relations.txt` and `qids.txt`. The committed river fixture is not refreshed (R-6); the water-body files are added beside it.
- **A station's body is identity, then geometry.** No station source publishes a QID, so a station belongs to a body by a curated `waterbody:` override or an exact published `water_name` in the body's `names`; polygon containment or the 500 m shore rule then decides, with the full geometry (never the simplified tile geometry); a point-only body is named inside its bbox. Distance alone assigns nothing.
- **A canal station snaps to its canal's edge.** This revises P17a's "canals reach no product": a canal station gets rule `canal_edge`, a `canal` field and no river, reach or chainage; canal lines have their own `canals` tile layer and are appended to the download (`layer: canal`), never in `rivers`. Canal ids still do not reach `station.river_id`, the API or `reaches-<ver>.json` `stations[]`.
- **Outputs.** Reaches `schema_version` 2 adds `waterbodies[]` (public stations only) and `mouths[]`; the tiles gain `waterbodies` and `canals`; the gate becomes 40 MB. The mouths of P17a name bodies of the file; `PENDING_WATERBODY_IDS` is gone.
- **Storage.** The station's body lives in the generated `registry/rivernet.yaml` and the column `station.waterbody_id` (table `waterbody`), never in the station registry files. The D27 reference gauge of every body is null until P18b.
