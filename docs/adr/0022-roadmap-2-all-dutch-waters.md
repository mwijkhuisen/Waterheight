# ADR-0022: Roadmap 2: from the inflowing rivers to all Dutch waters

- **Status:** Proposed (P15, 2026-10-16; issue #63); accepted when the owner merges P15. The plan is `docs/plan/PHASES.md` §54; the issues are #61 (roadmap 2), #62 (owner actions), #63 (P15), #64 (P16), #65 (P17), #66 (P18), #67 (P19), #68 (P20) and #69 (P21).
- **Date:** 2026-10-16
- **Revisit:** after the first storm-surge season on the wider map (Action D9, P20), or when the first water board grants a permission (P21), whichever comes first.

## Context

- **Roadmap 1 maps the rivers that flow into the Netherlands** (ADR-0012, ADR-0017). The owner decided on 2026-10-03 to extend the same map to the rest of the Rijkswaterstaat network (the lakes, the tidal delta, the canals, the coast, the Wadden Sea, the Scheldt estuaries and the offshore platforms), to sea level and to the 21 water boards (#61).
- **Most of it is open data.** The recorded NL-2 WFS shows 247 live Rijkswaterstaat level locations and 92 more discharge locations that roadmap 1 does not register; all of it is CC0 and served by the NL-1 adapter that already runs (the exact counts are the generated inventory in catalogue §2.1). The astronomical tide is published up to the end of 2027 at 98 locations.
- **The delta is a network, not a tree.** The Noord, the Dordtsche Kil and the Spui connect the branches and the tide reverses their flow. The graph builder fails on any cycle, by design.
- **Lakes are not rivers.** The IJsselmeer and Markermeer are held at seasonal target levels, and wind piles their water against one shore while the other drops.
- **Sea level is mostly tide.** A coastal gauge swings 2 to 4 m a day; in a storm what matters is the surge, the measured level minus the astronomical tide. The astronomical tide lies in the future, and invariant 4 rejects timestamps more than 15 minutes ahead in `obs`.
- **The request budget is the bottleneck.** NL-1 asks one location per request, and roadmap 1 uses 373 of its own cap of 400 requests per hour. Rijkswaterstaat publishes no limit.
- **Water boards need permission.** Four state in writing that their data may be reused and none names a licence; Vechtstromen forbids reuse without permission; most state nothing; few publish live levels in a machine-readable form. The umbrella organisations have no public live feed, and their open map objects are CC-BY-SA, which is share-alike (catalogue §2.8).

## Decision

1. **A tidal zone in the graph (P17a).** Rivers flagged `tidal_zone: true` (the delta below the tidal limit, D31) may hold cycles; inside the zone edges carry `direction: none` and no travel time. Outside it the cycle check is unchanged (fatal `cycle`, naming the ways). Mouths are curated end points where a channel enters a water body or the sea. EU-Hydro agreement is scored outside the zone only; inside it the edges are reported. The delta is hatched on the map and not animated, and "follow the water" ends at a labelled water body (D31, P19).
   - *Note, 2026-10-18 (P17a, #65; this is the note on decision 1):* built as decided, with these points. The zone is the 14 channels named in ADR-0012's P17a amendment, with edge `direction: none` and no travel time; Het Scheur lies outside it as a directed connector with a whole-river `tidal` flag (hatched, no arrows). The Hollandse IJssel id is `hollandse-ijssel`. Zwarte Water is a root river, and the Vecht hangs under it. Canals are edges (`kind: canal`, direction `none`) but reach no public output until P17b. Six mouths exist, naming the placeholder water bodies of `PENDING_WATERBODY_IDS`. EU-Hydro lists the `none` edges as `unscored`. Details in ADR-0012 and `docs/plan/PHASES.md` §54 (P17a as built).
2. **Lakes are water bodies (P17b).** `registry/waterbodies.yaml` holds lakes and closed estuaries as polygons, and the open sea and the tidal estuaries as point-only bodies unless OSM has a usable polygon (never hand-drawn without review). A station snaps to a body by containment or within 500 m of the shore plus a name, Wikidata or curated match, never by distance alone. A lake is coloured only by the class of **one named reference gauge** (D27), the other gauges are drawn as their own points, and the spread across the lake is information, never a state and never an average.
   - *Note, 2026-10-19 (P17b, #65; this is the note on decision 2):* built as decided, with these points. `registry/waterbodies.yaml` holds 23 bodies: 19 polygons (17 lakes, the Haringvliet as a closed estuary, the Oosterschelde) and 4 point-only (Noordzee, Waddenzee, Westerschelde, Eems-Dollard). No station source publishes a Wikidata item, so "name, Wikidata or curated match" became an exact published name or a curated override; Wikidata is the body's own evidence. The 500 m shore rule uses `waterbody_max_m` (at most 2000) only on an override. The reference gauge (D27) is null for every body until P18b (owner, 2026-10-19). A canal station snaps to its canal's edge (rule `canal_edge`), and canal lines have their own tile layer and download entries. Reaches schema 2 and the 40 MB tile gate follow. Details in ADR-0012's P17b amendment and `docs/plan/PHASES.md` §54 (P17b as built).
3. **Surge is a derived value, never stored as an observation (P18a).** Surge = measured − astronomical, taken at the measured value's own timestamp and in one datum (NAP, or MSL offshore; the two never meet in one subtraction). A missing astronomical value at that instant gives `null`, never an interpolated one. It is computed (an at-T function pair or the publisher, chosen in P18a with an ADR-lite line), carries `derived: "surge"` in every contract and is labelled "afgeleid, geen officiële klasse" / "derived, not an official class" on the map. Tidal water is never interpolated, in time beyond the staleness limit or in space (D12, extended).
4. **The astronomical tide has a store of its own (P16b).** It never enters `obs`: it is stored either through the P8 run model as a run of kind `astronomical` or in a table of its own (P16b chooses, with an ADR-lite line). Its readers get views in both families through `scripts/gen-views.ts`, and only `apps/server/src/db/audience.ts` names them. The measured and astronomical high and low waters are stored beside it and never enter the map snapshot as a station value. An alert fires when the stored horizon drops below 60 days.
   - *Note, 2026-10-17 (P16b, #64; this is the note on decision 4):* the choice is a table of its own, `tide`, with the view pair `pub_tide` and `own_tide`, not the run model (A§6 has the ADR-lite line: the 2,000-point cap, the immutable monthly re-fetches and the earliest fetch winning, against a curve whose newest fetch should win). It also holds the measured and astronomical high and low waters. The high or low label is **derived** by strict alternation in each payload list, because the payload has no marker; a list that breaks it is the retained drop `hwlw_unlabelled` and stores nothing (the owner, 2026-10-17: RWS does publish a type series, `Typering GETETTPE`, but it is captured later, issue #174). The alert is `astro_horizon`. No new dependency (`docs/plan/PHASES.md` §54, P16b as built).
5. **Water boards only by permission (P21; D28).** Each board feed has its own source ID, NL-7 to NL-26 (NL-5, Rijn en IJssel's Nexus layer, keeps its ID; NL-6 stays as the superseded placeholder), registered in P15 as `off` with no channel and no capture spec. A grant moves a board to `public` (with `registry/permissions/<ID>.md`), to `owner` (personal, non-commercial use only, with a `private_basis`; D22, ADR-0017) or leaves it `off`. A board's viewer, app or portal is never scraped, and an open endpoint is not consent. Each grant is a small PR; the Method page lists the boards still uncovered (D32), generated from the registry.
6. **The Rijkswaterstaat cap goes from 400 to 1,000 requests in any 60 minutes (D25, P16a; the owner raised it from the default 900 on 2026-10-17, because the D25 tiers did not fit 900: estimated about 976, built 946)**, at the cadence tiers 10, 30 and 60 minutes, only ≥ 10 working days after the notice C14 and only if Rijkswaterstaat has not objected; it is cut back at once if it asks. `budget.test.ts` keeps asserting it, keeps roadmap 1's specs alone at 400 or below and at most two concurrent requests to the host.
7. **Everything else of roadmap 1 holds:** the stack, the data model, all 11 security invariants, the audience rules, the PR workflow and the model rubric. No new runtime dependency is planned; the fetch hosts of Rijkswaterstaat are unchanged and a board's host is allowlisted for its own spec only, after its grant.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Scraping board viewers, portals or apps.** Several boards run a map viewer or an app over a platform that has an open endpoint (ArcGIS, Lizard, HydroNET). An open endpoint is not consent: of the boards researched on 2026-10-03, four state in writing that their data may be reused and none names a licence, one forbids reuse without permission and most state nothing. Rejected (D28): every board is asked (C16 to C36) and stays `off` until it answers.
- **`OphalenLaatsteWaarnemingen` in the core loop.** One call would return the latest value of many locations and save most of the request budget. The catalogue (§2.1) records that it runs one or two values late and ignores `ProcesType`, so a measured value could not be told from a forecast or an astronomical one. Rejected for the core loop; the one-location `OphalenWaarnemingen` stays, and the question of a multi-location endpoint is asked in C14.
- **Storing the astronomical tide in `obs`.** Invariant 4 rejects a timestamp more than 15 minutes ahead in `obs`. Rejected: a store of its own, with views in both families.
- **A lake as one value (an average of its gauges, or the nearest gauge).** Wind set-up makes one shore high and the other low, so an average or an unnamed gauge would mislead. Rejected for one named reference gauge and a visible spread.

## Consequences

- **The public map shows more than rivers**, and the tagline changes (D24): "Waterstanden van Nederland en de rivieren die het binnenstromen" / "Water levels of the Netherlands and the rivers that flow into it".
- **More requests to Rijkswaterstaat**, about 1,000 per hour at peak (946 as built; 986 in the worst case in which the three tide specs run at once), announced first (C14) and capped by a test. A refusal or objection means slower tiers, not a code change.
- **Two new stores and one derived value** to review at the edges: the water-body registry, the astronomical-tide store, and the surge (instants, datums, the missing value). During a storm surge the public reads these numbers, so P18 and the P20 release gate are reviewed by the strongest model (`docs/plan/PHASES.md` §3).
- **The delta has no travel time and no flow animation**, by design; the map says so.
- **Water boards arrive slowly and unevenly.** The map keeps showing the German and Belgian gauges upstream and the Rijkswaterstaat points until a board says yes; the Method page lists what is still uncovered. A grant that is later withdrawn needs a purge (P21 writes the procedure per board).
- **Share-alike data stays out of the stack** until a reviewed decision: Het Waterschapshuis' open map objects are CC-BY-SA 4.0, and the station data must stay a separate collective database.
- **The audience rules and the owner view are unchanged:** a board that allows personal use only is an `owner` source used by the owner alone, never shared (ADR-0017); the new `off` sources store nothing and appear in no public output.
- **P15 itself is documents, a generator and `off` registry rows,** with no capture spec, migration or dependency. It runs ahead of roadmap 1's launch by the owner's choice of 2026-10-16 (`docs/plan/PHASES.md` §54.1); P16 to P20 wait for the launch.
